How it works

Where the yield comes from.

A voyage has a cost to run and an invoice at the end of it. Orrith funds the cost up front and is repaid from the invoice. The operator pays for that capital. That payment is the investor return.

STEP 1

Capital pooled

Investors commit stablecoins to a fixed-term vault. Each vault has one purpose and one repayment date.

STEP 2

Voyage financed

Orrith advances the funds to the operator against the voyage invoice. Insurance and guarantees are in place before anything moves.

STEP 3

Cargo delivered

The ship sails, the cargo reaches port, and the buyer pays the invoice on delivery.

STEP 4

Capital returned

The lock ends and investors are repaid capital plus the fixed return, in the same stablecoin they deposited.

The economics

Why an operator pays for this.

A voyage operator needs the full cost of the trip, fuel, port charges, crew and cargo, before any revenue arrives. Today many operators raise that from several private lenders per trip, each taking a cut, each with their own terms.

Funding the whole voyage from one source, at a lower all-in cost, is worth paying for. The operator keeps more margin and control of the trip. Investors earn the financing charge. Orrith takes a fee for originating, structuring and reporting.

The return on each vault is fixed at launch and comes from the voyage's own economics, not from token emissions, leverage or trading.

Structure

Issuer, vault, investor.

ISSUER

Catalysis International, Panama

A special-purpose issuer holds the receivable and the related insurance and guarantees. It is the legal counterparty to investors.

VAULT

Established tokenisation rails

Orrith does not write its own vault contracts. Products are deployed on audited, established infrastructure with KYC-gated access.

INVESTOR

Stablecoins in, stablecoins out

Investors subscribe after KYC, deposit stablecoins for a fixed term and are repaid on a fixed date. Monthly reporting in between.

What every vault must have

Our underwriting rules.

  • A named obligor. We know who pays the invoice and on what terms.
  • Defined collateral. The invoice, the cargo, or the vessel, stated in the documents.
  • Insurance before funding. No capital moves until cover is confirmed.
  • A fixed term. One repayment date, known at launch.
  • Operator skin in the game. The operator funds part of the voyage itself and shares any loss.
  • A document pack. Obligor, insurance, legal structure and cash-flow timeline, available to every investor.
